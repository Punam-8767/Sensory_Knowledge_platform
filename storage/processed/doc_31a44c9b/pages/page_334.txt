A conﬁdence interval is a range of values within which the true value of a parameter lies
with a known probability. Conﬁdence intervals allow the researcher to determine if the
point estimates are sufﬁciently precise to meet the needs of an investigation.
Three types of conﬁdence intervals are presented: the one-tailed upper conﬁdence
interval, the one-tailed lower conﬁdence interval, and the two-tailed conﬁdence interval.
The equations for calculating these intervals for both m and p are presented in Table 13.4.
In general, two-tailed conﬁdence intervals are most useful, but if the analyst is only
interested in an average value that is either “too big” or “too small,” then the appropriate
one-tailed conﬁdence interval should be used.
